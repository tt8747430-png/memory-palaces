# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web (an installable PWA, phone first)

## Users

Anyone who needs to remember their own material: students and test-takers, language learners, professionals preparing
for a certification, people learning for themselves. What they share is wanting recall that lasts, and coming back day
after day if studying feels rewarding.

They study on a phone, in short bursts (a commute, a break, a few minutes before bed), often without a reliable network.
The job: turn notes or vocabulary into knowledge that sticks, and keep the habit long enough for spaced repetition to
work.

## Product Purpose

Mindscape turns material into lasting memory through spaced repetition, inside a light game that brings learners back.
Learners keep Folders of Decks, Decks nest as Subdecks, and a Deck holds Cards and Questions. They study in Practice
modes: Flashcards graded by the SRS (or reviewed fast), a Quiz over the Questions, and Match. XP, Levels, Streaks, a
daily goal, Achievements and Badges track and reward the work.

Everything works on the device, as a guest or signed in. With an account, Sync copies the learner's data to the cloud
and to their other devices, by hand or by Autosync. Extensions add optional features behind a switch in Settings; the
first is Bible, which turns a verse reference into Cards.

It exists because the tools that got the retention science right (Anki) are unpleasant to use, and the pleasant ones are
shallow. Success is a learner who studies daily and remembers more: streaks kept, training days counted, Cards reaching
Mastered.

## Brand Personality

Calm and premium on the surface, quietly game-like underneath. Careful spacing, soft daylight-blue glass surfaces and one
typeface (Lexend) should make it feel built with the care of Linear or Things, not like a toy. The game rewards real
study and pulls the learner back tomorrow, in moments (a celebration, a filling ring, a streak kept), not as constant
noise.

The voice encourages without being cute and is confident without being clinical: motivating like Duolingo,
progress-legible like Apple Fitness, and as calm as Notion or Bear while the learner reads, writes or reviews.

## Positioning

The study app that makes spaced repetition a habit: Anki's retention science, Quizlet's range of study modes,
Duolingo's daily pull and the polish of a premium consumer app, in one offline-first place.

## Anti-references

Clutter. Never a wall of controls, spreadsheet density, or so many options that the learner hunts for the one thing they
came to do. Depth sits behind progressive disclosure. When a screen feels busy, remove something rather than rearrange
it.

## Design Principles

Craft is the credibility. Every screen should look considered enough that someone used to Linear or Things would trust
it.

The game serves retention. A game element earns its place by bringing the learner back and rewarding real study; one
that does neither is clutter.

One job per screen. Everything else goes behind progressive disclosure.

Offline first. The app works fully with no network, and on-device data is the source of truth. The learner never waits
on a server to study.

One vocabulary. The same component means the same thing and the same action looks the same on every screen, in code and
in copy ([glossary](docs/UBIQUITOUS_LANGUAGE.md)).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast (body text at least 4.5:1, large text at least 3:1) in both the light and the dark theme; dark is a
palette of its own, not an inversion. Reduced motion is honoured app-wide at the token layer, from the OS setting or the
learner's own switch in Settings, and must stay that way.
